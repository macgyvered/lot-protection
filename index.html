For property owners across Portsmouth, Virginia

Protect your lot,
permanently.

Put a private rule on your own land that no future owner, and no developer, can undo.

This page helps you create a legal document that keeps narrow, cramped houses off your property for good. Read the short overview, fill in your details, and print a finished document ready to sign and file at the courthouse. It takes about ten minutes.

1 Understand what you're doing

What this document does

It is a deed restriction, a rule you choose to record against your own property. Once filed at the courthouse, it travels with the land and binds every future owner.

  • It sets a hard minimum on side yards. No house can be built on your lot with side yards smaller than the width you choose (7 feet by default). That keeps out the skinny, 3 foot setback builds.
  • It outlasts the zoning board. Even if the city grants someone a permit for narrow setbacks, your private rule is stricter, and the stricter rule still holds. The city will not cancel it.
  • It makes your lot far less useful to a developer. A lot that cannot be built narrow is often a lot a flipper walks away from.
  • You stay in control. The rule only limits skinny building and subdivision. It does not touch how you live in your home, and you can still garden, fence, and maintain your property.
A note on tax benefits: this plain restriction gives protection but no tax break. If your lot is vacant and you would consider keeping it as permanent open space, a conservation easement or an outright land donation can protect it and may earn a Virginia tax credit. That is a larger step with its own rules. You can research it yourself through the Virginia Department of Conservation and Recreation and the Virginia Outdoors Foundation, or ask a real estate attorney.

Your information

Before you begin: have your deed handy

You will need the full legal description of your property, which is written on your recorded deed. Don't have your deed? Get a copy from the Clerk of the Circuit Court, which keeps all recorded deeds (copies cost about 50 cents per page). This is the same office where you will record your finished document:

    The rules you're setting

    Turn on the rules you want. Pick any one, two, or all three, and leave off the rest. At least one is required.

    Current Portsmouth zoning limits (single-family, for reference). What the City allows depends on your zoning district:
    • NR / GR (Neighborhood or General Residential): side yard 10 ft · height 36 ft · coverage 45%
    • UR (Urban Residential): side yard 7 ft · height 36 ft · coverage 55%
    Other districts (UR-M, historic, and downtown) differ. Confirm your district and its current numbers in the Portsmouth zoning code (Sec. 40.2-218). Your private rule can be stricter than these limits, but not looser: the City's minimum always applies on top of your rule.
    Purpose: keep a house from being built right up against the side lot line, crowding the homes next door and cutting off their light, air, and drainage. Set the smallest side yard you want to guarantee, and use at least your district's minimum (7 ft in UR, 10 ft in NR/GR). Enter a whole number of 7 or greater.
    Caps how tall any house can be. Most existing homes on these blocks are one or two stories. Portsmouth's UR zoning currently allows up to 36 feet, so either option below is stricter.
    “Coverage” is the share of your lot covered by buildings (house, porches, decks, sheds), not counting driveways. Portsmouth's UR zoning currently allows up to 55%, which is what lets a builder fill a small lot wall to wall. A lower cap keeps yard space, light, and drainage. On a typical 3,200 sq ft lot: 35% is about 1,120 sq ft of building, 40% about 1,280 sq ft, 45% about 1,440 sq ft.
    Enter a whole number. To actually restrict building, it must be below your district's current maximum (for example, 55% in UR). Avoid a number below the footprint of your existing house, or the rule could make your own home too large. Enter a whole number between 5 and 70.

    How to file it

    This page is for you. Keep it. Do not hand this instruction page to the Clerk. Record only the Declaration above, plus the cover sheet from Step 3.
    1. Print it on plain white paper. Use 8.5 by 11 inch white paper, single sided, black ink. Do not sign it yet.
    2. Sign in front of a notary. Bring a photo ID. Every owner listed must sign. Notaries are available at many banks, UPS Stores, and law offices. The notary fills in the bottom section.
    3. Make the required cover sheet. Portsmouth requires a Virginia Land Record Cover Sheet on every filing. Generate one free from the city's Deeds and Land Records page, print it, and place it on top of your document.
    4. Record it with the Clerk. Take the signed, notarized original plus the cover sheet to the Clerk of the Circuit Court (the same office that keeps recorded deeds) and pay the recording fee (based on the number of pages). Confirm the current hours before you go.
      Clerk of the Circuit Court (City of Portsmouth)
      1345 Court Street, Suite 101, Portsmouth, VA 23705-1217
      Confirm current phone and hours on the city or Virginia Courts website.
    5. Keep your stamped copy. The Clerk returns a recorded copy. Keep it with your important papers. The protection is now permanent and public.
    Free bonus: sign up for the Clerk's VA Deed Alert to get an email any time something is recorded against your name or parcel. It is an easy way to catch unwanted activity early.
    Before you record: have a Virginia real estate attorney or other real estate professional review your finished document. A quick check now prevents a rejected filing.
